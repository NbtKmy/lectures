Plane rectangular coordinates vs WGS84

WGS84 / JGD2011 latitude–longitude

EPSG:4326 (WGS84) · EPSG:6668 (JGD2011)

  • A geographic coordinate system: positions on the curved Earth, in degrees.
  • One system for the whole world. GPS, Google Maps and most downloads use it.
  • A degree is not a fixed distance: one degree of longitude is about 100 km in Okinawa but only about 80 km in northern Hokkaido.
  • For Japan, WGS84 and JGD2011 latitude–longitude agree to within a few centimetres. You can treat them as the same for most maps.

JGD2011 plane rectangular zones

EPSG:6669 … EPSG:6687 (zones I–XIX)

  • A projected coordinate system: the Earth is flattened onto a plane, positions are in metres.
  • Japan is split into 19 zones. Each has its own origin (X = 0, Y = 0) and is accurate to about 1 part in 10,000 inside the zone.
  • Used for surveying, municipal and cadastral data, and any analysis that measures distances or areas.
  • Only valid near its own area: using zone VII for Hokkaido gives distorted shapes and distances.

Why metres matter in QGIS

Tools such as Buffer, Distance matrix and the $area and $length expressions use the units of the layer's CRS. In EPSG:4326, a buffer of “500” means 500 degrees, which is larger than the Earth. Reproject the layer to the zone for your area first, and 500 means 500 metres.

X is north, Y is east

In Japan's plane rectangular coordinates, X points north and Y points east — the reverse of the usual maths convention. A table of survey points with columns X and Y usually needs X as the northing and Y as the easting. If your points appear in the sea or rotated by 90°, swap the two columns when you load the CSV.

JGD2000, JGD2011 and Tokyo Datum

JGD2011 replaced JGD2000 after the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake moved eastern Japan by up to several metres. Older data may use JGD2000 (EPSG:2443–EPSG:2461) or the much older Tokyo Datum, which is offset by about 400–450 m. Check the CRS in the metadata of the data. Don't guess.

When to use UTM instead

If your study area spans several zones, as most of Kyushu or all of Japan does, one UTM zone (JGD2011 / UTM zone 51N–55N, EPSG:6688–EPSG:6692) or an equal-area projection is usually the better choice.

Set it in QGIS

  1. Project CRS: Project ► Properties… ► CRS, search for the EPSG code, for example 6675, and click OK. The current project CRS is shown at the lower right of the status bar.
  2. Check a layer's CRS: open the layer's Properties ► Source tab.
  3. Convert a layer: right-click the layer ► Export ► Save Features As…, choose the zone's CRS and save a new file. Or use the Processing tool Reproject layer.
  4. Careful: Layer ► Set CRS of layer(s) only changes the label. It does not convert the coordinates. Use it only when the data has no CRS, or the wrong one, and you know the right one.

Worked example: Moriyama, Nagoya

Moriyama Ward is in Aichi, so it is in zone VII (第VII系), EPSG:6675. In the hospital-access exercise of the QGIS session, set the project to EPSG:6675 and reproject the hospitals and roads layers to it before buffering. A 10-minute walk can then be set as a distance in metres.
