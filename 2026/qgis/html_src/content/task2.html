Task 2 · A 10-minute walk from one hospital

Goal: answer one question — how many people live within a 10-minute walk of one hospital? The hospital is Kawashima Hospital (川島病院) in Moriyama Ward, Nagoya. About 15 minutes. Here the presenter opens a finished project and walks you through it: switch layers on and off, click objects, read the numbers.

The layers

All six files are in the repository. To follow along, drag them into a new QGIS project.

  • moriyama_admin_field.gpkg — the ward boundary.
  • moriyama_hospital_test.gpkg — one point: 川島病院.
  • moriyama_service_area_lines.gpkg — the roads you can walk along within 10 minutes (walking speed 4 km/h).
  • moriyama_test_10min_polygon_clean.gpkg — the 10-minute reach as an area.
  • moriyama_mesh_3km.gpkg — the 250 m population grid (2020 Census) around the ward: 2,745 cells.
  • moriyama_test_10min_intersection_with_population_clean.gpkg — only the grid cells inside the reach, cut to its edge: 20 pieces, each with an estimated population pop_est.

The tour

  1. Switch on the ward boundary and the hospital point. Zoom to the hospital.
  2. Switch on the walkable roads. These are all the streets reachable on foot within 10 minutes. This is the line version of the answer.
  3. Switch on the 10-minute reach. It is a smoothed surface of walking time over the road network, cut at 10 minutes. It turns the lines into an area.
  4. Switch on the population grid, then the 20 pieces inside the reach. Choose the Identify Features tool and click a piece: pop_est is the estimated number of people living in that piece.
  5. Open the Basic statistics for fields tool on the 20 pieces, field pop_est. The sum is 5,420.9 — about 5,421 people live within a 10-minute walk of 川島病院.

How the number is made

A grid cell that is only half inside the reach gets half of its population. This is called area apportioning. It assumes the people in a cell are spread evenly across it.

The first run of this analysis gave about 6,878. Grid cells outside the reach had slipped into the sum. After the fix the answer fell by about 21%. It came to light only when the reach outline looked doubled on the map.

Not the same as Task 3. This reach was built with a different method from the one in Task 3. Do not compare the two numbers.

Watch out

  • Even spread inside each cell is an assumption. A cell with one apartment block and a park is not even.
  • The reach depends on the walking speed (4 km/h) and on which roads count as walkable.
